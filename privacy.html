TRUST — PRIVACY POLICY

Privacy policy

This policy covers two things. Part one is this website, vision-guard.org. For this website, we decide what happens to the details you send us. Part two is the VisionGuard software once it is installed at a customer's premises. There, the organisation running it controls the data, and we provide the software. We describe what happens today, including what is not built yet.

Who we are

VisionGuard makes the VisionGuard camera software and runs this website. We are based in Egypt.

  • Data protection contact, for privacy and terms questions and requests about your data: support@vision-guard.org
  • Sales: sales@vision-guard.org
  • Phone or WhatsApp: +20 100 253 5081, Sunday to Thursday, 9:00–18:00 Egypt time

Website — when you visit

This website is a set of plain pages. It has no accounts, no database and no server code of its own, and we keep no record of your visit. Two outside services do see each visit:

  • Hosting: the website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs the IP address of every visitor for security. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • Fonts: each page loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, so your browser also sends a request to Google. That request includes your IP address. See Google's Privacy Policy.

The website sets no cookies and uses no browser storage (such as local storage). It has no analytics and no advertising, and we do not follow you from page to page or across other websites.

The WhatsApp, email and phone links send nothing until you tap them. Once WhatsApp opens, WhatsApp's own policy applies.

Website — the two forms

We collect only what you send through our two forms:

  • Send us a message (home page), sent to sales@vision-guard.org: your name, work email and company. Your phone and a message, if you add them. Also the type of site, picked from a list, and the number of cameras, picked from four buttons. Each starts with a choice already made, so both are usually sent even if you do not change them.
  • Ask us a question (Help centre), sent to support@vision-guard.org: your name, email, the topic you pick and your question.
  • Each form has a hidden field that stops spam robots. It collects nothing about you.

We use what you send only to answer you. For a sales enquiry, we also use it to continue the conversation you started about VisionGuard. We do not sell your details, and we do not add you to any mailing list or send you marketing messages.

Legal basis: you send the form so that we reply. That is your consent for this purpose. For a sales enquiry, it is also a step you asked for before a possible contract. For the visit data described above, the basis is our legitimate interest in running a working, secure website.

If the page cannot confirm that your message was sent, it says “not sent”, keeps what you typed and shows our email and phone. If JavaScript is off in your browser, the form opens FormSubmit's own page instead, and FormSubmit's policy applies there.

Website — who receives your details

Your browser sends the form straight to FormSubmit, which emails it to us. ImprovMX, an email-forwarding service, then forwards it to our team's mailbox at Google (Gmail). We use these services only to deliver the website and your message. Apart from them, we do not share your details with anyone, unless the law requires it.

  • FormSubmit: receives your message with your IP address and browser details, and keeps its own copy of submissions. See FormSubmit's privacy policy.
  • ImprovMX: forwards the email to our mailbox. See ImprovMX's privacy policy.
  • Google: holds our mailbox (Gmail) and serves the fonts. See Google's Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub: hosts the pages. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Outside Egypt: when you visit, your IP address reaches GitHub and Google. When you send a form or email us, your message passes through FormSubmit, ImprovMX and Google. GitHub, Google and ImprovMX are based in the United States, and ImprovMX also uses servers in Europe. FormSubmit's privacy policy does not say where it stores submissions. So your details are processed outside Egypt. For your message, we rely on your consent, given when you choose to send it. If you would rather your message did not leave Egypt, call us on +20 100 253 5081 instead.

Website — how long we keep your message

We keep your message in our mailbox for 12 months after our last reply to you. Then we delete it. If you ask, we delete it sooner.

This covers our own copy. FormSubmit keeps its own copy of submissions; we have not found a published period for how long. ImprovMX, Google and GitHub keep their own records under their own policies. We do not control those copies.

Product — who is in charge of the data

The VisionGuard software runs on a computer at the customer's own premises. The organisation running it decides what the cameras cover, whom to enrol for face recognition, which features to switch on and who receives alerts. That organisation controls the data its installation holds and is responsible for it under the law. VisionGuard provides the software.

The software has no built-in way to report back to us or to let us in remotely. What an installation sends out goes through the Google accounts entered on that installation, as described below.

If you are seen or recorded at premises that use VisionGuard, and you want to see, correct or delete your data, ask the organisation that runs it. It holds the data. If you contact us instead, we will point you to it.

Product — what leaves an installation

An installation keeps its records on its own machine, and sends no video or picture to any outside service. Only two features send any of its records out, and both go through Google, using the accounts entered on that installation:

  • Ask: before a question goes anywhere, VisionGuard replaces two kinds of words with placeholders, on the installation's own machine: the names of people enrolled there, and the plates on its vehicle register. It then sends the question to Google's Gemini, together with the installation's area and group names, to work out what is being asked. Any other word is sent exactly as typed, for example the name of someone who is not enrolled, or a plate that is not registered. VisionGuard is set up today for Gemini's free tier. On unpaid services, Google's Gemini API terms let Google use what is submitted to improve its products, and let human reviewers read it. A paid Gemini account changes what Google does with the text, not what is sent. If Ask is switched off, or no Gemini key is set up, nothing is sent.
  • Email alerts: email is off until an administrator switches it on for each kind of alert and adds recipients. Messages go out through Gmail as plain text, with no attachment. VisionGuard never puts a face, a picture, or the name of a person it recognised into a message; camera and group names appear as the administrator typed them. A message can contain the camera, the time, how sure the system is, a vehicle's plate, a gate number, group names with attendance counts (in the noon report), the name of the staff member who reviewed a knife alert, and, if the installation has set one, a link to its console. A copy of each message also stays in the Sent folder of the Gmail account the installation sends from.

Google processes Ask questions and alert emails outside Egypt. The organisation running VisionGuard decides whether to switch these features on, and is responsible for any licence and consent Egyptian law requires for sending this data abroad.

Two other things reach Google but carry no records. The Ultralytics software library that runs VisionGuard's detection models sends usage statistics to Google Analytics, at most about once every 30 seconds while the models run on a machine with internet access: the model file name, the computer's processor and graphics card, software versions, and how many images were processed and how fast. And the console and the phone app load a typeface from Google Fonts when opened. Neither sends pictures, names or records, but Google sees the IP address of the installation or of the viewer.

Product — what it keeps, and for how long

VisionGuard does not record video all the time. On the installation's machine it keeps:

  • People enrolled for face recognition: name, type (staff, student or visitor), group, an optional detail such as a class, the date enrolled, and face data, which is a set of numbers made from their photos. The photos themselves are not stored. Kept until an administrator removes the person.
  • Attendance and sightings: who was recognised, on which camera, when, and how sure. No video. No deletion schedule today.
  • Track a person: on cameras where this feature is switched on, the position of every person seen, noted about every 3 seconds and linked to a name when their face is recognised; and the stops on each named person's path. This covers everyone on those cameras, not only people someone searches for. No deletion schedule today.
  • People counting: the number of people in each area, once a minute, with no identity. No deletion schedule today.
  • Knife alerts: the video is deleted after 30 days by default (each installation can change this). The entry itself (time, camera, confidence, who reviewed it and what they decided) is kept for good, false alarms included.
  • Restricted zones: each camera keeps its three most recent clips, whatever their age. The entry (camera, start and end time, highest number of people) is kept for good.
  • Vehicle gate: the photo and plate close-up are deleted after 30 days by default. The pass record (time, gate, direction, plate) is kept until an administrator deletes that pass, which also removes its pictures. The vehicle register (owner's name, phone, unit, car details, permit and notes) is kept until an administrator changes it. Deleting a register entry keeps that car's past passes.
  • Recordings started by hand from a camera (up to 10 minutes each), including recordings of a person being followed: not deleted automatically.
  • Track a person playback: video prepared for playing back a person's path is a working copy. While VisionGuard runs, it keeps the last three per camera; copies left from earlier runs are not deleted automatically. Older video comes from the recorder already on the premises (DVR/NVR), under that recorder's settings.
  • Alert emails: the recipient list (name, email, language) and a delivery log. Log entries are deleted after 90 days by default.
  • Who dealt with each “Needs your attention” item, and when: no deletion schedule today.
  • User accounts: name, email, role, language, last sign-in, and a one-way hash of the password, never the password itself. The product has no way yet to disable or delete an account.
  • The log of who searched or changed what, including each Ask question exactly as typed: held only in the program's memory, and lost when it restarts. Enrolling a person, exporting attendance and signing in are not in this log. (The program's own window shows the email and IP address of each sign-in attempt and new account while it runs; this is not saved to a file.) Making this log permanent is planned but not built.
  • Lost bag: keeps no video, picture or history.

Automatic deletion runs only while VisionGuard is running. If it is switched off, or a file is open elsewhere when its time comes, the file can stay past its date.

Removing a person deletes their directory entry and face data at once, and they stop being recognised from the next camera frame. Their past sightings, movement records and path stops stay in the database, and log entries naming them stay until the program restarts. A way to erase those too is planned but not built.

Your rights

Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 151 of 2020) gives you the right to:

  • know what personal data is held about you, see it and get a copy;
  • withdraw your consent;
  • correct, update, complete or delete your data;
  • limit its use to a specific purpose;
  • be told of any breach affecting your data;
  • object to how it is used, where that conflicts with your fundamental rights and freedoms.

For data we hold from this website, email support@vision-guard.org from the address you used on the form, or call us. We will reply within the time the law sets. For data held in a VisionGuard installation, ask the organisation that runs it.

You can complain to Egypt's Personal Data Protection Center at any time: www.pdpc.gov.eg, info@pdpc.gov.eg. This does not affect your right to go to court.

Children and face data

This website and its forms are meant for adults acting for an organisation. We do not knowingly collect data from children through them. If you are under 18, please ask a parent or guardian to contact us for you.

Under Egyptian law, face data and any data about a child are sensitive personal data. Processing them needs a licence from the Personal Data Protection Center and, unless the law expressly allows otherwise, explicit written consent: for a child, a parent's or guardian's. An organisation that uses VisionGuard must arrange this itself, before it enrols anyone. VisionGuard does not collect consent on anyone's behalf.

Security

This website holds no personal data of its own. It is served only over HTTPS, and http:// addresses redirect to it. Our copy of form messages is kept in our team's mailbox.

In the product, passwords are stored only as a one-way hash, and the server decides what each account may do. Anyone who can reach the console on the customer's network can create their own account. It is always the everyday (IT) kind, never an administrator, but it can see live cameras, names, attendance and movement history.

Today an installation's records are stored on its machine without encryption, the product has no backup feature, and the console is reached over a plain connection (not HTTPS) on the customer's own network. The organisation running it should protect that machine and network like any system holding this kind of data. More on the Security page.

If we learn of a breach of personal data we hold, we will report it to the Personal Data Protection Center and tell the people affected, as the law requires.

Changes and governing law

If we change this policy, we will update this page and the date on it.

This policy is governed by Egyptian law, including Law No. 151 of 2020 and its Executive Regulations. Disputes about it go to the competent courts of Cairo, unless Egyptian law gives another court jurisdiction. This does not limit your right to complain to the Personal Data Protection Center.
